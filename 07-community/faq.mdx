### **1. What is Nexio? Is it similar to DeFi lending?**

Nexio is a Bitcoin-based credit platform that lets verified institutions lend and borrow BTC at fixed rates.

All loan terms, payments, and accounting happen on-chain, giving participants full transparency into how BTC moves and earns yield.

Nexio differs from DeFi lending in a few key ways:

- **Verified participants:** Only KYC/KYB-approved institutions can lend or borrow.
- **Fixed terms:** Each Series offers a defined duration and yield instead of variable rates.
- **Bitcoin network:** All BTC stays in Taproot Vaults on the Bitcoin network.
- **Legal enforceability:** Each loan is backed by signed agreements, not anonymous smart contracts.

### **2. What is a Series or the Master Vault?**

A Series is a ring-fenced, fixed-term BTC credit pool with its own borrower set, coupon rate, and maturity date. Each Series operates independently (similar to a bond issuance) so its risk and yield are isolated from others. Lenders choose a Series based on duration and return preferences, earning a predictable BTC-denominated yield until maturity.

Borrowers have their own dedicated Series (1 Series per borrower).

The **Master Vault** serves lenders who prefer automated diversification instead of selecting individual Series. It aggregates BTC across multiple active Series, giving single, blended exposure to Nexio’s overall credit portfolio while keeping all funds secured in Taproot Vaults.

### **3. What are uBTC and yBTC?**

When a lender deposits BTC into a **Series**, they receive **uBTC**: a tokenized receipt that represents their proportional ownership of that Series.

uBTC automatically reflects both principal and accrued yield, similar to a fixed-term note that increases in value until maturity.

Depositors in the **Master Vault** receive **yBTC**, a yield-bearing token that tracks the blended performance of all active Series.

Both uBTC and yBTC are fully backed 1:1 by BTC held in Taproot Vaults, ensuring transparent, verifiable custody on the Bitcoin network.

### **4. Who can lend or borrow on Nexio?**

**Lenders** are verified institutions such as Bitcoin treasuries, funds, family offices, and corporate balance sheets.

**Borrowers** are KYC/KYB-verified trading desks, market makers, or arbitrage firms that pass eligibility and collateral checks.

Both sides operate under enforceable agreements and continuous monitoring.

Retail participation may be introduced in the future through regulated partners or managed vaults.

### **5. How is BTC custody managed?**

Custody is fully **Bitcoin-native**: all BTC remains on the Bitcoin network inside Taproot Vaults, and all vault balances and signer actions are independently verifiable on-chain.

Each vault uses a 2-of-3 threshold Schnorr signature scheme shared between Custody, Validation, and Governance operators.

Every Series has its own dedicated vault, isolating capital and preventing any cross-series exposure. Routine vault actions (such as borrower draws or coupon payments) are executed through multi-party co-signing.

Transactions use Taproot key-path spends, which compress multi-signature logic into a single, standard Bitcoin transaction for efficiency and privacy.

### **6. What is Proof Gating?**

Every mint, burn, or redemption of uBTC/yBTC is executed only after a confirmed Bitcoin transaction proof is received.

This ensures that the tokenized supply on Nexio always matches the verifiable BTC in custody. Nothing moves until Bitcoin confirms it.

### **7. How do BTC borrowers earn yield?**

Borrowers use BTC from each Series to run institutional trading and liquidity strategies that generate predictable returns.

Three examples include:

- **CME basis trading:** capturing the spread between Bitcoin spot and futures prices.\
  Borrowers lock in this difference by buying spot BTC while shorting futures, earning the premium as futures converge to spot at expiry.
- **Delta-neutral funding:** hedging market exposure while earning funding premiums.\
  They maintain a neutral position (long and short BTC in equal amounts) so profits come from funding rate differentials rather than price moves.
- **Market-making:** providing BTC liquidity across exchanges for trading fees and spreads.\
  This involves quoting both buy and sell orders, earning small profits by selling slightly above and buying slightly below the market price.

Each borrower operates under a fixed-term loan agreement with strict collateral and reporting requirements, and pays interest back to the Series vault at defined intervals.

### **8. What happens if a borrower defaults or breaches a covenant?**

If a borrower fails to meet payment terms or violates a Series covenant, that Series automatically enters liquidation mode.

Collateral is priced, sold, or transferred in accordance with predefined recovery rules, thereby minimizing losses and containing them within the Series.

The Operator Queue oversees the process, validating every liquidation event and updating Series balances in real time. All recovery actions, shortfalls, and repayment metrics are recorded on-chain, providing full visibility to governance participants, auditors, and lenders.

### **9. How is compliance enforced?**

Every participant passes KYC/KYB and AML checks before transacting.\
Withdrawals require a compliance attestation from a registered handler.

Each Series stores hashed legal agreements (Loan, Pledge, Term Sheet) in contract metadata to ensure verifiable legal linkage.

### **10. What networks does Nexio support?**

Custody remains on Bitcoin, while uBTC and yBTC exist as ERC-20 tokens on Ethereum, with plans to expand to other networks.

Mint/burn events are cryptographically tied to Bitcoin proofs, keeping supply consistent across networks.
