---
title: "Borrower Tiers"
description: "A description of Nexio's 3 types of borrowers."
---

Borrowers are classified into one of three tiers:

1. \*\*Tier 1: \*\*Regulated trading firms with audited balance sheets and direct CME access.
2. **Tier 2:** Established desks or market-makers with exchange connectivity and a proven track record.
3. \*\*Tier 3: \*\*Emerging borrowers or higher-volatility desks.

This categorization is decided by Nexio's risk and governance committee and is based on factors such as credit quality, operational history, and collateral strength. Typically, it is clear which tier a Borrower should fall under.

The tier determines how much BTC a firm can borrow, how frequently it reports, and which strategies it can access. As an example, a Tier 1 borrower posting \$11 M in collateral might be able to borrow up to \$10 M in BTC, while a Tier 3 borrower with the same collateral wouldn't be able to draw quite as much.

### Tier 1 Borrowers

Tier 1 borrowers are **regulated trading firms and market participants** with audited financials, direct CME access, and long operating histories.\
They maintain high transparency standards, low leverage, and strong risk controls.

These firms are eligible for the lowest-risk Series (like CME Basis and Delta-Neutral Funding) and enjoy lower collateral ratios and faster approvals due to proven counterparty strength.

### Tier 2 Borrowers

Tier 2 borrowers are established trading desks and market-makers with active exchange connectivity, track records of profitability, and institutional operations, but without the full regulatory stature of Tier 1 firms.

They typically engage in moderate-risk strategies, such as cross-exchange market-making or arbitrage, and operate under tighter utilization caps and higher collateral ratios than Tier 1 borrowers.

### Tier 3 Borrowers

Tier 3 borrowers are emerging or higher-volatility trading firms, often smaller in size or pursuing more aggressive strategies.\
They may lack long-term audits or operate in less regulated jurisdictions, so they face the strictest collateral and reporting requirements.

Tier 3 borrowers can access higher-yield Series, such as active market-making or directional credit, but must post substantially more collateral and report activity more frequently to maintain eligibility.
