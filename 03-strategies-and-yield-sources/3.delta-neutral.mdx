---
title: "Strategy 2: Delta-Neutral Funding"
---

The Delta-Neutral Funding strategy captures the funding-rate differential between long and short perpetual futures positions across exchanges. A perpetual future refers to a type of futures contract with no expiry, such as an indefinite 3x long BTC position.

This strategy is designed to generate steady BTC-denominated yield from market imbalances, not from directional bets on Bitcoin’s price.

On Nexio, this strategy is for **Tier 1 and Tier 2 borrowers**, who can benefit from funding payments that occur between traders taking opposing positions on perpetual swaps

Because funding rates fluctuate based on market sentiment (positive when longs dominate, negative when shorts dominate), borrowers can **earn the funding payments** by holding offsetting positions — one long, one short — that neutralize price risk.

### **Example: 0.03% funding rate**

Assume BTC trades at \$100,000, and a perpetual futures contract on Exchange A pays a +0.03% hourly funding rate to short positions.

The following could occur:

1. The borrower **buys BTC spot** on Exchange B.
2. The borrower **shorts BTC perpetual futures** on Exchange A, hedging the price exposure.

The position is **delta-neutral**, meaning BTC price movements cancel out between the two legs.

For instance, if BTC’s price rises to \$105,000, the borrower’s spot position gains \$5,000, while the short futures position loses \$5,000 (netting zero directional P&L).

Meanwhile, the borrower earns the 0.03% hourly funding paid by overleveraged long traders on Exchange A.

### **Why It Works**

Perpetual futures markets are dominated by speculative traders, often creating funding-rate imbalances. When sentiment is bullish, funding becomes positive and shorts get paid; when bearish, longs get paid.

Borrowers on Nexio position themselves on the paying side to harvest these imbalances continuously, while keeping market exposure hedged.

For lenders, this strategy offers higher yield potential than CME Basis Series and fully collateralized exposure with real-time monitoring of borrower positions
