---
title: "Strategy 3: Market Making"
---

The **Market-Making** **strategy** operates by providing BTC liquidity across exchanges and capturing the spread between buy and sell orders. When a borrower draws BTC from this Series on Nexio, they deploy it across trading venues to earn small, repeated profits from market activity (similar to how high-frequency trading firms operate in equities or FX).

On Nexio, this strategy is used primarily by **Tier 2 and Tier 3 borrowers**, as market-making carries moderate operational and market risk compared to fully hedged strategies like CME Basis.

The strategy requires deep exchange connectivity and efficient risk management. Borrowers typically hedge inventory across venues to keep exposure neutral while continuously earning micro-returns on trading volume.

### **Example: \$50 BTC spreads**

If BTC trades around **\$100,000**, a borrower might:

1. Place buy orders at **\$99,950** and sell orders at **\$100,050**.
2. When both orders fill, the borrower earns **\$100 in spread profit** (\$50 per side).
3. Repeating this process thousands of times per day generates steady BTC-denominated income.

If market volatility spikes, the borrower widens spreads to maintain profitability while staying neutral in exposure. The borrower’s trading income (after fees and hedging costs) funds the fixed coupon owed to the Series vault, with any excess yield above the Series rate remaining with the borrower.

### **How It Works on Nexio**

This might work the following way on Nexio:

1. **Borrower Draws BTC: **The approved market-making firm borrows BTC from the Series vault under a fixed-rate, short-tenor Series (typically 30–90 days).
2. **Deploys on Exchanges: **The borrower uses the BTC to provide liquidity across centralized and decentralized venues, earning spread income and maker rebates.
3. **Periodic Coupons: **A share of earned BTC income flows back to the Series vault at scheduled intervals as coupon payments, which increase the uBTC share price for lenders.
4. **Repayment and Settlement: **At Series maturity, the borrower repays principal plus any remaining BTC yield, verifiable through on-chain accounting and signed confirmations.
